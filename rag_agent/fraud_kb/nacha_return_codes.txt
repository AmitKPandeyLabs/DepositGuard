NACHA ACH Return Codes — Fraud-Relevant Reference
DepositGuard Compliance Knowledge Base

OVERVIEW

NACHA (the National Automated Clearing House Association) defines standardized return
codes that a Receiving Depository Financial Institution (RDFI) uses to send an ACH entry
back to the Originating Depository Financial Institution (ODFI) when it cannot or will not
post the entry as submitted. Return codes fall into several categories: administrative
(bad account data), NSF/funds-related, and unauthorized/disputed-authorization codes. The
codes below are the ones most relevant to fraud investigation and risk scoring.

R01 — INSUFFICIENT FUNDS
Description: The available balance in the receiver's account is not sufficient to cover
the dollar value of the debit entry.
Fraud risk level: LOW. R01 is a routine funds-availability return, not an indicator of
unauthorized activity. High R01 frequency on an account can indicate financial distress or
mismanagement, but on its own is not a fraud signal. It becomes relevant to fraud
monitoring only when paired with unusual account velocity or a pattern of large debits
immediately following deposits (potential check-kiting or first-party fraud).

R09 — UNCOLLECTED FUNDS
Description: A sufficient ledger balance exists, but a sufficient collected (available)
balance does not exist, typically because recently deposited funds have not yet cleared.
Fraud risk level: LOW. Similar to R01, this is primarily an operational/funds-timing
return. Repeated R09 returns combined with rapid redeposit-and-withdrawal cycles can be a
weak signal of check-kiting or deposit fraud, but is not itself indicative of unauthorized
transaction fraud.

R05 — UNAUTHORIZED DEBIT TO CONSUMER ACCOUNT USING CORPORATE SEC CODE
Description: A consumer's account was debited using a corporate (CCD) Standard Entry Class
code rather than a consumer-authorized code, and the receiver states the debit was not
authorized.
Fraud risk level: HIGH. R05 is a direct unauthorized-debit assertion and is one of the
strongest ACH-side fraud signals. It frequently correlates with account takeover, business
email compromise feeding fraudulent origination, or a fraud ring using a compromised
account/routing number pair. R05 returns should route to fraud investigation, not routine
operations queues.

R07 — AUTHORIZATION REVOKED BY CUSTOMER
Description: The receiver has revoked authorization previously provided to the originator
for a specific recurring or single debit entry.
Fraud risk level: MEDIUM-HIGH. R07 typically reflects a legitimate authorization dispute
(e.g., a canceled subscription still being billed) but is also used when a consumer
disavows ever having authorized a transaction in the first place, which shades into
unauthorized-debit territory. Distinguishing "revoked legitimate authorization" from
"denies ever authorizing" requires investigator review of the consumer's statement; treat
clusters of R07 on a single account, or R07 following a sudden account-detail change, as
elevated risk.

R10 — CUSTOMER ADVISES ORIGINATOR IS NOT KNOWN TO RECEIVER AND/OR NOT AUTHORIZED
Description: The receiver claims they do not recognize the originator and/or never
authorized the originator to debit their account.
Fraud risk level: HIGH. R10 is the consumer-account counterpart to R29 and is a core
unauthorized-debit signal, commonly seen in account-takeover fraud, identity theft used to
originate debits against a victim's account, and fraud-ring test transactions. A single
R10 warrants review; multiple R10 returns tied to the same originator or the same
receiving account within a short window is a strong escalation trigger.

R29 — CORPORATE CUSTOMER ADVISES NOT AUTHORIZED
Description: The corporate/business receiver states the debit entry was not authorized,
the business-account analog of R10.
Fraud risk level: HIGH. R29 often indicates business email compromise (BEC), a
compromised treasury/payments workflow, or an insider-originated fraudulent debit. Because
commercial accounts typically carry higher transaction limits, R29 returns should be
treated as high-severity and escalated promptly, with attention to whether the same
origination credentials produced other suspicious entries.

USING RETURN CODES IN RISK SCORING

For account-level fraud risk scoring, unauthorized-debit codes (R05, R10, R29) should
carry substantially more weight than funds-availability codes (R01, R09). R07 sits in
between and should be weighted based on frequency and context (isolated R07 = likely
legitimate dispute; recurring or account-change-correlated R07 = elevated risk). None of
these codes alone proves fraud — they are signals to be combined with other behavioral and
account-opening features (device/identity mismatch, velocity, account tenure) in a
composite risk assessment.

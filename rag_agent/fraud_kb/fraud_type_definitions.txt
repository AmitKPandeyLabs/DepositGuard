Deposit Account Fraud Type Definitions and Behavioral Signals
DepositGuard Compliance Knowledge Base

OVERVIEW

This reference defines the primary fraud typologies relevant to deposit account
origination and servicing, and lists the behavioral signals investigators and models use
to distinguish each type. These typologies are not mutually exclusive — a single account
can exhibit signals from more than one category (e.g., an account opened via new-account
fraud is later used as a mule account).

NEW ACCOUNT FRAUD

Definition: Fraud committed at account origination, where the applicant uses a stolen,
synthetic, or fabricated identity to open a deposit account with no intent of legitimate,
ongoing use. The goal is typically to obtain a credit line, receive fraudulent deposits
(e.g., counterfeit checks or stolen funds), or establish an account for money-laundering
purposes before the fraud is detected.

Key behavioral signals:
- Low name-to-email similarity or other identity-verification mismatches at application.
- Use of a free/disposable email provider combined with a newly created email address.
- High velocity of applications sharing a device fingerprint, IP address, or partial
  identity attributes within a short time window (zip_count_4w, velocity_6h/24h/4w style
  features).
- Short or nonexistent address history (low prev/current address tenure).
- Request for a credit limit disproportionate to the stated income or credit risk profile.
- Application submitted very rapidly after the originating session/request began (low
  days_since_request), suggesting a scripted or bulk-application pattern rather than an
  organic customer journey.
- Invalid or unverifiable phone contact fields (phone_home_valid / phone_mobile_valid
  both false).

ACH FRAUD

Definition: Fraud involving the ACH network — either unauthorized debits pulled from a
victim's account (often following account takeover or stolen banking credentials), or
fraudulent origination of ACH entries by a bad actor who has gained access to origination
credentials.

Key behavioral signals:
- NACHA unauthorized-debit return codes on the account: R05, R10, or R29 (see
  nacha_return_codes.txt), particularly multiple occurrences or occurrences clustered
  around a credential or contact-detail change.
- Sudden change in the account's registered contact details (email, phone, device)
  immediately preceding disputed transactions.
- A burst of ACH transaction volume inconsistent with the account's established pattern.
- Return-code churn (a mix of R07 disputes following what looks like account compromise
  rather than simple subscription cancellations).

CHECK FRAUD

Definition: Fraud involving forged, altered, or counterfeit checks, or check-kiting
schemes that exploit the float between deposit and clearing to withdraw funds against
checks that later bounce.

Key behavioral signals:
- Rapid deposit-then-withdrawal cycles: a check is deposited (often via remote deposit
  capture) and a large share of the funds is withdrawn or transferred out before the
  check has had time to clear.
- Deposited check amounts clustered just under manual-review or holds thresholds.
- Checks drawn from payers with no apparent relationship to the account holder, or from
  accounts that themselves show fraud indicators.
- Elevated R01/R09 (insufficient/uncollected funds) return activity in combination with
  large, rapid outbound transfers — a pattern consistent with check-kiting across
  multiple accounts.

MULE ACCOUNT FRAUD

Definition: An account used to receive and quickly move illicit funds on behalf of a
fraud or money-laundering operation, whether the account holder is a knowing participant
or an unwitting victim recruited through a job, romance, or investment scam. See
bsa_overview.txt for the related regulatory (SAR) obligations.

Key behavioral signals:
- Multiple incoming transfers from unrelated third parties shortly after account opening,
  with no apparent personal or business relationship to the account holder.
- Rapid pass-through behavior: funds received are withdrawn, wired, or transferred out
  within hours to a few days, leaving minimal residual balance (low bank_months_count /
  bank_branch_count activity paired with high transaction velocity).
- Transaction volume grossly disproportionate to the account holder's stated income,
  occupation, or credit-risk profile at origination.
- Shared device, IP, or session characteristics with other flagged accounts, suggesting
  coordinated fraud-ring activity (device_distinct_emails_8w, device_fraud_count).
- Newly opened account (low bank_months_count) with an immediate spike in transaction
  activity rather than the gradual ramp-up typical of legitimate usage.

USING THESE DEFINITIONS IN INVESTIGATION

When an account is flagged for review, investigators should map the account's top
risk-driving features (e.g., from a SHAP explanation) to the typology whose behavioral
signals best match the observed pattern, then consult the corresponding regulatory
guidance (Regulation E for unauthorized-transfer handling, NACHA return codes for
ACH-specific signals, and the BSA/AML overview for SAR and mule-account obligations) to
determine the appropriate investigative and reporting response.

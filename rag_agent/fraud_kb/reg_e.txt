Regulation E — Electronic Fund Transfer Act (12 CFR Part 1005)
DepositGuard Compliance Knowledge Base

OVERVIEW

Regulation E implements the Electronic Fund Transfer Act (EFTA) and governs electronic
fund transfers (EFTs) involving consumer accounts, including ATM transactions, point-of-sale
debit card transactions, ACH transfers, direct deposits, preauthorized transfers, and
person-to-person (P2P) transfers. It establishes the rights, liabilities, and responsibilities
of consumers and financial institutions for electronic transfers, and sets out the process
banks must follow when a consumer reports an error or an unauthorized transaction.

SCOPE OF PROTECTED TRANSFERS

Reg E covers any transfer of funds initiated through an electronic terminal, telephone,
computer, or magnetic tape that instructs a financial institution to debit or credit a
consumer's account. This includes debit card purchases, ATM withdrawals, ACH debits and
credits, online bill pay, and P2P payment network transfers (e.g., Zelle) when routed
through a participating financial institution. Wire transfers and checks are generally
excluded from Reg E and are governed by other frameworks (UCC Article 4A, Regulation CC).

ERROR RESOLUTION PROCESS

When a consumer notifies the institution — orally or in writing — of a suspected error
(including an unauthorized transfer), the institution must:

1. Investigate the alleged error promptly.
2. Complete the investigation within 10 business days of receiving the notice of error.
3. Report the results to the consumer within 3 business days after completing the
   investigation, and correct the error within 1 business day of determining an error
   occurred.

45-DAY EXTENDED INVESTIGATION AND PROVISIONAL CREDIT

If the institution cannot complete its investigation within the standard 10-business-day
window, it may take up to 45 calendar days total to investigate, provided it:

1. Provisionally credits the consumer's account for the disputed amount (less any
   applicable hold) within 10 business days of the notice of error (20 business days for
   accounts open less than 30 days).
2. Notifies the consumer of the provisional credit within 2 business days of providing it.
3. Gives the consumer full use of the provisionally credited funds while the investigation
   continues.

The 45-day window extends to 90 calendar days for: (a) transfers initiated outside the
United States, (b) point-of-sale debit card transactions, and (c) transfers involving new
accounts (open less than 30 days).

If the institution determines no error occurred, it may reverse the provisional credit,
but must first: send a written explanation, provide copies of the documents relied upon
if requested, and give the consumer 3 business days' notice before debiting the
provisionally credited funds back out of the account.

UNAUTHORIZED TRANSFER LIABILITY LIMITS (CONSUMER)

Consumer liability for unauthorized EFTs is tiered based on how quickly the consumer
reports the loss or theft of an access device or discovers unauthorized activity:

- $50 maximum liability if the consumer notifies the institution within 2 business days
  of learning of the loss, theft, or unauthorized transfer.
- $500 maximum liability if the consumer fails to notify the institution within 2
  business days but does notify within 60 days after the statement showing the
  unauthorized transfer was sent.
- Unlimited liability if the consumer fails to report the unauthorized transfer within
  60 days of the statement date, for any subsequent unauthorized transfers that
  reasonable notice would have prevented.

These liability limits apply to unauthorized transfers involving an access device (e.g., a
compromised debit card or online credentials used by a third party). They do not apply to
transfers the consumer themselves authorized, even if the consumer was later defrauded
into authorizing them (e.g., certain authorized push-payment / social-engineering scams),
which is an evolving area of interpretation and enforcement guidance.

ACH DISPUTE PROCESS

For ACH-specific disputes, Reg E error-resolution timelines apply on the receiving
depository financial institution (RDFI) side once a consumer disputes a transaction:

1. Consumer contacts the bank and asserts the ACH debit was unauthorized or in error.
2. The bank opens a Reg E claim and begins the standard 10-business-day investigation
   clock (or invokes the 45/90-day provisional-credit extension described above).
3. Separately, under NACHA Operating Rules, the RDFI may initiate an unauthorized-entry
   return (commonly R05, R07, R10, or R29 depending on the fact pattern) back through the
   ACH network within 60 calendar days of the settlement date for consumer accounts, to
   recover funds from the originating institution.
4. The Reg E investigation and the NACHA return process run in parallel; the Reg E
   provisional-credit obligation to the consumer is not contingent on successfully
   recovering funds through the ACH return.

DOCUMENTATION AND RECORD-KEEPING

Institutions must retain records of Reg E error claims, including the consumer's notice,
the investigation findings, and any correspondence, for a minimum of 2 years from the date
the institution was required to comply. These records are a primary source institutions
must be able to produce for examiner review and for internal fraud-investigation audit
trails.
